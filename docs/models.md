# Models

Bamah doesn't have a model of its own. You connect the ones you want, and pay the service directly for what you use, which for writing scenes is usually a lot less than a subscription.

## Connecting one

In **Settings**, under **Models**, tap **Add** and choose:

| | What you need |
|---|---|
| **OpenRouter** | one key from openrouter.ai, which opens hundreds of models from every company. The easiest start. |
| **OpenAI**, **Claude**, **Gemini** | a key from that company. |
| **Ollama** | nothing: a model running on your own computer, free and private. |
| **Other** | the address of anything else that speaks the OpenAI format (LM Studio, a server of your own). |

When you add your first one, Bamah asks which of its models your characters should use.

**Your ChatGPT plan:** under **ChatGPT**, **Continue with ChatGPT** signs in with your own ChatGPT account, and its models then appear like any other. On your own computer the sign-in finishes by itself. On a server, the ChatGPT tab ends on a page that won't load, and you paste that page's address into the box Bamah shows you, once.

## Which one, for what

There are three jobs, under **Settings › Default**:

- **Characters:** the voices. This is where a better model shows: the bigger, newer ones keep a character's voice and their contradictions much better. It's worth trying a few.
- **Memory and turn order:** the quiet work, like writing memories, tracking feelings and deciding who speaks next. A small, quick model is fine here, and costs very little.
- **Portraits:** an image model, only if you want to generate portraits. **Generate** asks for one the first time. See [Portraits](portraits.md).

A story can have its own model (**Setup › Model**), and so can a character (their **Model** page), which wins over the story's. That way the old general can speak through one model and the poet through another.

## Thinking

Most of the newer models think before they answer. That thinking is paid for like the reply itself, and it's why some of them sit quiet for a while before the first word shows up. A few think as hard as they can unless they're told otherwise. **Settings › Default › Thinking** says how much they should, for models on OpenRouter:

- **Low**, to start with: a little thought, a quick reply, and a much smaller bill.
- **Off**, for the quickest and cheapest. A model that can't stop thinking altogether thinks as little as it can.
- **High**, when a scene matters and you don't mind waiting and paying more. If the lines feel flat, it's worth trying.

It's for the characters' lines. Memory, feelings and who speaks next never think more than **Low**, since they happen after every line. If a model won't take the setting, Bamah asks it again without, so a line never fails over it.

## Comparing them

The quickest way to find the model that sounds right is the crossed arrows under a line: they take the same line again with whichever model you pick. Every take is kept, and **‹ 1 / 2 ›** steps between them. The model that wrote a line is shown in grey next to the character's name.

When a model refuses, runs out of credit, or the key is wrong, the line says so in red instead of the reply.
