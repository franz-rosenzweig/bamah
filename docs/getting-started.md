# Getting started

This page takes you through your first story, step by step: a model, two characters, a story and a scene. It's the same walk the tour gives you inside Bamah. The first time you open Bamah on a device, a card in the bottom corner offers the tour: **Start** takes you through it, pointing at each thing as you go, and **Skip** leaves it for later. Either way, it's always there in **Settings › Tour**, and you can stop it any time with **Skip tour**.

![The card that offers the tour](images/start-01-invite.webp)
*The card that offers the tour, the first time Bamah opens.*

## 1. Add a model

Your characters need a model to speak through. Bamah doesn't come with one, because you pick the service and pay it directly, so the first step is in **Settings**, under **Models**.

![Settings, with Models highlighted](images/start-02-model.webp)

Tap **Add**, choose the service and paste its key. OpenRouter is the easiest start, because one key opens almost every model there is. [Models](models.md) has the other choices, including your ChatGPT plan and a model on your own computer.

![Adding OpenRouter with its key](images/start-03-add-model.webp)
*Adding a connection: the service along the top, then its key.*

When you save it, Bamah asks which of its models your characters should use. You can change that later, and give any story or character a different one.

![Choosing the default model](images/start-04-pick-model.webp)

## 2. Make your first character

Go to **Characters** and tap **New character**.

![Characters, with New character highlighted](images/start-05-new-character.webp)

You don't have to fill in a blank sheet. Write a few words about who they are, like "proud, funny, runs her late mother's kitchen, never apologizes first", and tap **Draft**. Bamah writes a first draft of the whole sheet: who they are, their inner life, how they talk and what they always remember. It's a starting point to argue with, so change anything that isn't them.

![A few words in the Draft box](images/start-06-draft.webp)

Give them a name. Their pronouns, whether they speak as "I" or in the third person, and their style are right under it.

![The Name row](images/start-07-name.webp)

## 3. Give them a face

The square button under the portrait opens the library: every portrait you've made or uploaded, and any public-domain ones added to your Bamah (mine has several hundred, paintings and old photographs, see [Portraits](portraits.md)). Tap one and **Use**. You can also **Upload** a picture of your own, or **Generate** one from their appearance if you've picked a portraits model.

![The portrait library](images/start-08-library.webp)
*The library, with every portrait you can use.*

![Mara with her portrait](images/start-09-face.webp)

The boxes in the middle are the character. The more specific you are in them, the more they sound like themselves. See [Characters](characters.md) for what each one does.

## 4. Make someone for them to talk to

Make a second character the same way: **New character**, a few words, **Draft**, a name and a face.

![The Characters page with the first character](images/start-10-second.webp)

## 5. Start a story

Go to **Stories** and tap **New story**.

![Stories, with New story highlighted](images/start-11-story.webp)

Give it a title at the top and a premise. Everyone in the story knows the premise, so it's the place for what the world is and what's going on.

![The story's title and premise](images/start-12-premise.webp)

Under **Cast**, tap **Add** and add both characters.

![The cast](images/start-13-cast.webp)

## 6. Start a scene

On the story's **Scenes** tab, tap **New scene**.

![Scenes, with New scene highlighted](images/start-14-scene.webp)

Give the scene a title, say where and when it happens, choose who's in it, and **Start**.

![A new scene: title, setting and who's in it](images/start-15-scene-sheet.webp)

## 7. Set it going

Write what happens, as the narrator: someone walks in, a letter arrives, a question is asked. Then send it.

![The first line, written as the narrator](images/start-16-first-line.webp)

The story decides who would answer and they answer in turn. If your line names someone, they answer first. To bring someone else in, tap their face under the scene.

## 8. Read what they don't say

Every reply starts with a line in lilac: what the character feels and keeps to themselves. It shapes how they answer, and only you can see it. The other characters never do.

![The private line above a reply](images/start-17-inside.webp)
*Mara's private line, above what she actually says.*

## 9. When a line isn't right

Under the newest line are its buttons. The circle arrow takes the line again. The thumbs down lets you write what they'd really do, and can keep it as a correction so they don't do it again. The crossed arrows try the same line with another model. [Stories and scenes](scenes.md) has every button.

![The buttons under a line](images/start-18-fix.webp)

## 10. Choose who speaks

Tap a face to have that character answer next, or **Go on** to let the scene decide. Writing your next line works too.

![The faces under the scene](images/start-19-speakers.webp)

## 11. See how they feel

After every line, each character's mood and their feelings toward the others are updated in the **Feelings** panel. The bars show how strongly they feel it. On a phone or a smaller screen, the heart at the top opens it.

![The Feelings panel](images/start-20-feelings.webp)
*Mara is coiled and furious with Ben. Ben is cornered and a little afraid of her.*

## 12. Try something without consequences

**What if** starts a scene that's a sketch. Nothing in it is remembered, until you decide it counts.

![What if](images/start-21-whatif.webp)

## 13. End the scene

When the scene is over, end it from the menu at the top. Each character writes down what they'll remember of it, from their own point of view, and takes that into the next scene. See [Feelings and memory](feelings-and-memory.md).

![The scene menu](images/start-22-end.webp)

![The tour's last card](images/start-23-done.webp)

That's the whole stage. From here it's your story.
